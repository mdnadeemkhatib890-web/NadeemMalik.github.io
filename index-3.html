Most contract problems don't show up at signing. They show up during a funding round, a co-founder exit, or a payment dispute. By then, fixing them is slower and costs more.

Checklist: IP assignment in freelancer agreements

If a freelancer or agency builds any part of your product, the agreement should cover these points.

  1. ScopeAll code, designs, documentation and work in progress, including drafts.
  2. Rights assignedAll rights, present and future, in every medium.
  3. DurationStated expressly ("in perpetuity"), since a blank period can default to a limited term under the Copyright Act.
  4. TerritoryStated expressly ("worldwide"), since silence can default to India only.
  5. Moral rightsA waiver, or an undertaking not to enforce them against you.
  6. Third-party and open-source codeA warranty that the freelancer may use it, plus a disclosure list.
  7. Further assurancesA promise to sign any documents needed later, such as for a funding round.
  8. Payment linkClear terms on when the assignment takes effect.

This checklist is general information, not legal advice. Every engagement is different.
